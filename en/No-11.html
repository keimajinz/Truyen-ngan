#11

On a chilly evening in early autumn, Vũ was startled to realize that he had lived through another long year without the people who had once breathed in time with him. Six counts in, eight counts out. Ten fingers relaxing, letting go of memories already worn halfway through. Green leaves had turned yellow, yellow had deepened to red, and the heart that had once been red had quietly taken on a shade of blue.

Vũ sighed. His steps had grown slower than ever, yet his blurred eyes could no longer keep pace with the world as it changed around him. He had grown used to the low hum of the chimney outside, the steady whir of his computer left running. He had even forgotten the noise of traffic that once carried all the way to the bedroom at the back of the house.

They say it takes only sixty-six days to form a habit. How long had it taken him to begin longing for this tiny room and its cheap mattress? Vũ closed his eyes and felt the warmth of a pair of arms gently embracing him from behind.

“What are you thinking about?”

“Nothing,” Vũ replied.

He stroked the soft black hair as it nuzzled into his shoulder, and let his thoughts drift with the breeze. Blue had seeped into his present—a deep blue, like the ocean beneath the stillness of the night—its hue drawn from the soul of a young man whose name he cannot call.

“There’s nothing left for me to teach you,” Vũ murmured.

The arms around his waist tightened a little. Silence settled between them. Then a large hand gently covered his eyes and coaxed him down onto the bed.

“So you don’t want me here anymore?”

The voice was calm, almost matter-of-fact, as though Vũ had never been the one with the right to decide.

Vũ did not answer. He simply turned and moved closer to the graceful figure lying beside him, fitting himself against the young man’s body. He set his eyes aside, to let his other senses take over, finding in each touch, in each lingering layer of scent, a more immediate sense of the life that was his in this moment. The familiarity of their closeness lulled him towards sleep.

Somewhere between waking and dreaming, he found himself marveling at the presence of someone whose hand he could hold as they stepped together into the dream. As though all the troubles that came with daylight could be left for tomorrow. As though the distance between their souls were slowly dissolving into illusion.

The hands soothing his body carried away the question, which had begun to stir in him: would he ever be able to sleep like this again, sinking beneath the horizon alongside the sun?

***

Sunlight flooded the room. Vũ woke at neither an early nor a late hour, only to find that his little puppy was no longer beside him.

Click. The bedroom door opened, and a cheerful greeting came before he had the chance to complain.

“You’re awake? Perfect timing. Go get ready, or we’ll be late.”

“Where did you go?” Vũ asked sleepily, squinting at the young man, who was already dressed and ready to leave.

“I went out to get some bread for breakfast. I’m making your coffee now.”

“Come here,” Vũ called, still half asleep.

He reached out and caught the young man as he approached. Warmth and a gentle, familiar scent enveloped him. He lingered in the embrace for a few moments before finally getting up to wash his face.

Click. The bread sprang from the toaster, golden and crisp, its fragrance spreading through the room. Ice clinked in a glass of milk coffee, mingling with the dry scrape of a knife spreading jam across the crust. The morning opened quietly, bright and clear.

Why are we nothing to each other?

Vũ stared, bewildered, at the young man’s back. It was a sight so familiar, and yet he could not understand how it had become so. The bitterness of the coffee touched his tongue, gentle as sweetness. Swimming through the dark waters of memory, he began to understand, dimly, why they had never given their relationship a name. The answer, like so much else between them, remained indistinct, something that existed only as a feeling.

“Come on,” the young man called. “Let’s go.”

Where are we going? What will we do? Why is he here with me, in a room meant for one? Where does he live? How old is he?

The questions that should have come naturally never quite reached the present. Vũ put on his coat, slipped into his shoes, and took the young man’s hand. Together, they stepped through the door, stepping out of this world.

***

A cold breeze brushed the tip of his nose, and Vũ opened his eyes.

This time, when he woke up, the young man was still in his arms. Vũ shivered and drew the blanket over his face.

“You’re awake?” came a whisper from beneath the covers.

Before he could answer, he felt a soft warmth against his hand, then his chest, his shoulder, and finally his neck. He startled slightly, then relaxed, his eyelids drooping as he sank once more towards sleep.

His boy was so endearing. Gentle, composed, familiar.

Vũ felt his heartbeat quicken. Perhaps that was what was slowly drawing him out of sleep. He opened his eyes a little and quietly studied the young man’s long, glossy black lashes, then let his gaze fall to those small, rosy lips.

“Aren’t you going?” he asked softly.

How strange. He did not even know what he was asking. He only felt that this boy was supposed to go somewhere, and that wherever it was, it could not be here beside him.

“No,” the young man replied, his voice firm with certainty. “I’m staying here with you.”

I’m staying here with you.

The words echoed in Vũ’s mind, accompanied by a bright, tinkling laugh, like little bells ringing in a steady rhythm, soothing the unease in his heart. He gazed, almost dazed, at the young man’s radiant smile as it seemed to dissolve into the sunlight, becoming honey that melted sweetly across his lips.

Vũ had not been here for very long, yet somehow he had grown accustomed to this simple life. The old desire to explore the world, to wander wherever his heart might take him, seemed to belong to another version of himself, one living in another universe. Here, more than anything, he wished for freedom for the person he loved.

He welcomed those gentle eyes that seemed to hold the whole world within them, and wished that this boy might discover even more beauty beyond the confines of this little life, out there in the vastness of the world.

He closed his eyes and pressed a light kiss to the young man’s hair. His fingers slipped into the soft, billowing strands, twining them around and letting them fall loose again. A faint herbal scent crept into his breath, and its quiet rhythm carried him towards tomorrow.

***

Rain pattered against the windows on an autumn morning. A pale blue washed across the cloudless sky. Vũ could not tell what time it was. He let out a soft groan, rolled over, and sat up. It was the weekend, and he had planned to clean the apartment.

Though he called it cleaning, there was not much to put in order in this little place. The dishes had been washed, the laundry done, the rubbish taken out regularly. All that remained was to mop the floor and sort through his wardrobe. Sweaters and long trousers would have to take the place of the T-shirts and shorts that had filled it through the summer.

Standing before the mirror, he noticed how pale the cold had made his lips. The scrape of his toothbrush mingled with the trickle of water as it warmed beneath the tap.

Then, without warning, a tender kiss landed on his tired shoulder. A hand reached past him to switch on the heater, and a layer of soft fabric brushed against his back.

“I’ve already swept and mopped the floor. Have some hot coffee, okay? It’s a little chilly today.”

Vũ did not reply. He simply reached for the hand at his side and laced their fingers together.

In the mirror, the young man looked bright and full of life. A blue-grey T-shirt and black sweatpants made for an ordinary outfit to wear around the house, yet did little to hide the strong, graceful lines of his body. His soft, tousled hair shimmered, its jet-black strands catching a deep blue sheen beneath the golden light. But beyond his gentle, sparkling eyes, it was his fluffy, silky hair, soft as a puppy’s fur, that Vũ loved most.

After brushing his teeth, he took a sip of coffee and opened the wardrobe. Leaning against the young man, he studied its contents for a long time.

Then, all at once, he realized that not a single thing in it belonged to his boy.

There was nothing here that the young man could call his own. His presence in Vũ’s life was no more than a fleeting shadow.

Vũ wanted to ask him about it, but could not bring himself to speak. So they continued tidying in silence, putting away the bright, flamboyant clothes of summer and replacing them with the thick, subdued layers of winter.

Perhaps that was how it would be for him, too. Perhaps the spring in his life would one day have to give way to the loneliness of autumn.

Ring, ring.

The phone rang shrilly. The young man frowned and walked towards the door to answer it. Something about the call made Vũ uneasy. There was a hint of displeasure on the young man’s face, just enough to make him suspect that something was wrong.

“I have to go take care of something,” the young man said after hanging up.

He reached for his coat, turned, and headed for the door.

Where are you going? When will you be back? Or could you just stay?

The words hovered on the tip of Vũ’s tongue, but none of them came out. He stood frozen, watching the young man disappear through the doorway.

A sudden unease swept over him.

His boy had lied. He would not be coming back.

Vũ did not know why he was so certain, but he believed it completely.

He bolted out of the apartment and raced up the stairs to the rooftop. He had no idea what was leading him there, yet he never once questioned the strange knowledge that had suddenly appeared in his mind.

He flung open the rooftop door.

In the distance, he saw the familiar figure growing smaller, walking between two tall, intimidating men. He hurried after them and seized the young man by the shoulder.

“Where are you taking him?” he shouted, pulling the boy tightly into his arms.

“Listen, sir. He can’t stay here any longer. Please don’t stand in our way,” one of the strangers said.

“What do you mean?” Vũ asked, bewildered.

The young man lay quietly in his arms, making no attempt to resist. Yet his face was filled with an unbearable sadness.

The two men stepped forward and seized him.

Vũ clung to the young man desperately, his voice breaking as he sobbed, “No! Give him back to me!”

He still could not understand what was happening. He searched for an answer in his boy’s gentle, doe-like eyes. In his confusion, he barely felt the tender hands stroking his face.

“I have to give you back to your own reality.”

Little by little, white spread across the sky, erasing the outlines of everything around them until nothing remained but the two of them, suspended in an endless void.

The last thing left in the world was the smile of the person he loved, sweet and bitter all at once.

And then Vũ was standing alone in his little room, with nothing but the memory of the young man, lingering like a midsummer night’s dream.

There was no regret, nor any grief at their parting. What had bloomed in his heart was something closer to wholeness, the quiet fulfillment of having lived through a brief but happy lifetime.

All that remained was a small fragment of doubt about whether love had ever truly existed at all.

For he and the young man had never once called each other by name.
